Use cases across government and commercial sectors.

Underwater

Saltenna is developing high-bandwidth stealthy communications underwater without needing line of sight.

Under Ice

In some of the world's most extreme environments, Saltenna will enable communications both through and along ice sheets, connecting above-ice and air assets with those submerged beneath.

Along Runways, Flightlines, and Roads

Saltenna is developing stealthy, resilient wireless communications along large, linear infrastructure.

Around Aircraft

With through-metal capabilities, Saltenna can enable persistent communications while aircraft are out on the flightline or in hangars.

In Dense and Collapsed Infrastructure

In unpredictable environments full of concrete, metals, and other blocking materials, Saltenna is working on consistent and robust wireless communications beyond radios and Wi‑Fi.

Underground, in Tunnels, and in Parking Garages

Saltenna has demonstrated significantly enhanced range over current wireless technology in solid underground facilities, tunnels, and through multiple levels of parking garages.

Inside Ships and Submarines

Rather than being blocked by metal bulkheads, Saltenna's Plasmonics leverage them to enable wireless communications. Saltenna plans to outfit vessels with wireless communications and new sensing systems without running cables.

Along and Through Metal for Industrial Internet-of-Things

In high pressure, high value IIoT networks, Saltenna has demonstrated wireless communications along and through pressurized tanks and pipes without drilling holes or running wires.

Along and Through Pipelines

Saltenna is working on wireless communications for pipelines above ground, underground, or underwater — including those filled with hazardous liquids.

Under and Through the Jungle Canopy

Where radios may fail, Saltenna is working on Plasmonics that can carry communications within and through dense jungle canopy to support commercial, military, law enforcement, and intelligence operations.

On the Lunar Surface and Beyond

Saltenna's simulations show that our communications technology could work in environments without an ionosphere, such as the moon and other planets, and that we could reduce the need for vacuum feedthroughs in equipment that complicate structural integrity in locations of extreme risk.

Through and along the ice.

A technology that behaves differently from all others.

Current radios, Wi‑Fi, Bluetooth, optical, audio, and other systems radiate through free space and fail at dense obstacles. Plasmonic waves instead travel along surfaces — the boundaries between water, metal, soil, and air — so no line of sight is needed, and the energy stays stealthy on the surface instead of broadcasting into contested spectrum.
